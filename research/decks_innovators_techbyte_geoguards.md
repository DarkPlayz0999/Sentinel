## Q1. Slide structure, order, headings and key lines (per deck, per slide)

### Takeaway
All three decks use the **official 6-slide SIH Idea Submission template in the same order**: (1) Title page, (2) Idea/Proposed Solution, (3) Technical Approach, (4) Feasibility & Viability, (5) Impact & Benefits, (6) Research & References. They differ only in how they fill it. None of them has a separate "problem" slide. The problem is folded into slide 2.

### Inferences
- The fixed template means judges compare decks **section-for-section**. The way to win is to fill the same six boxes better, not to reorder them. None of the three adds extra slides such as a team or demo slide.
- The problem is never given its own slide. Each deck puts "problem" framing inside slide 2, through emotive images (Innovators), an "Objective" line (Techbyte) or an "Addressing the Problem" card (GeoGuards).

### Gaps
- I could not independently confirm that these three teams were SIH **grand-finale winners**. The only claim comes from the repository's README/filenames. Techbyte's PS 1587 is a "Student Innovation" (open) PS, so it could have been an internal-hackathon or idea-stage selection. Treat "winning" as unverified.
- The empty "Technologies :" area on GeoGuards slide 3 may have held logos that were lost in capture. This cannot be determined from the PDF.

## Q2. Every diagram, flowchart and image: what it depicts, layout, encoding and intended meaning

### Takeaway
Innovators uses emotive **stock imagery plus a hand-built hub-and-spoke "APP" flow**. Techbyte uses a **radial mind map** and a **numbered hexagon roadmap**. GeoGuards has the richest visuals by far: a real **domain illustration (mine cross-section)**, a **5-step ML pipeline**, a **DATA → Low/Medium/High risk fan-out tied to actual prototype dashboard screenshots**, a **cause-breakdown pie chart with per-cause solution callouts**, and **research-paper 3D geological figures**.

## Q3. Thinking behind each deck: problem framing, hook, novelty/USP, feasibility justification, risks, impact, numbers

### Takeaway
None of the three decks uses hard quantitative evidence (no market size, no accuracy %, no cost in ₹). They win on **clarity of mapping to the PS**, a **named, tangible solution**, and, in GeoGuards's case, **visible proof of a working prototype**. Risks and mitigations are explicit only in Innovators. Impact is always framed as Social / Economic / Environmental, or by stakeholder.

### Cited Findings
- **Innovators hook and framing**: the hook is emotional, a rhetorical question ("ARE WOMEN SAFE IN INDIA ??") plus "STOP VIOLENCE AGAINST WOMEN" imagery. The USP is labelled explicitly as "#INNOVATION AND UNIQUENESS", covering gender-specific analytics, "Gesture recognition for SOS", real-time analytics, and hotspot insights. The features ("Gender distribution analysis", "Detection of a lone women at night", "Hotspots identification") track the BEL PS almost one-to-one. — [Innovators p2](file:///Users/himanshusolanki/Downloads/sih/SIH-Winners-PPt-and-Sources/SIH_2024_Innovators_High_Quality.pdf); [PS 1605 text](https://github.com/Wraient/SIH-2024-Problems)
- **Innovators feasibility and risks**: feasibility is only named ("Technical / Financial / Social feasibility") with no justification. Risks: "Privacy concerns, Data security and miss use, Technical challenges". Mitigations: "Privacy protection, Data security, Algorithm accuracy". Each is one noun phrase with no mechanism. — [Innovators p4](file:///Users/himanshusolanki/Downloads/sih/SIH-Winners-PPt-and-Sources/SIH_2024_Innovators_High_Quality.pdf)
- **Innovators impact**: split by stakeholder (women / law enforcement / urban communities) and also by Social / Economic / Environmental. There are no numbers. The only India-specific facts are the helpline numbers "181/100". — [Innovators p3, p5](file:///Users/himanshusolanki/Downloads/sih/SIH-Winners-PPt-and-Sources/SIH_2024_Innovators_High_Quality.pdf)
- **Techbyte framing**: an open "Student Innovation" PS is turned into a **named product, "AlertMe"**, with a crisp "Objective:" line. The USP is breadth: an all-in-one "Emergency Response Coordination Platform" combining data aggregation, communication, resources, crowd-sourcing and a dashboard. — [Techbyte p2](file:///Users/himanshusolanki/Downloads/sih/SIH-Winners-PPt-and-Sources/SIH_2024_Techbyte_High_Quality.pdf)
- **Techbyte feasibility**: the most business-like viability section of the three, covering "Market: Demand...; Stakeholders: Emergency teams, government, NGOs", "Partnerships & Funding: ... Grants, R&D funding, and crowdfunding". Technical feasibility is argued through the availability of APIs ("Feasible with APIs (weather, social media, IoT)"). A 4-stage build roadmap and a Figma prototype link add credibility. The reference list cites Indian and global authorities (sachet.ndma.gov.in, FEMA, GDACS), but also "openai.com/chatgpt" and "github.com", which is naive. — [Techbyte p3, p4, p6](file:///Users/himanshusolanki/Downloads/sih/SIH-Winners-PPt-and-Sources/SIH_2024_Techbyte_High_Quality.pdf)
- **GeoGuards framing**: the problem is framed as life-safety in a specific industrial setting ("preventing lifelosses"). The solution is honest about scope: "based on simulated weather datasets". The USP is stated on the references slide: "The YOLO algorithm, though widely used in object detection, is being adapted here in a unique way for predicting geological risks." — [GeoGuards p2, p6](file:///Users/himanshusolanki/Downloads/sih/SIH-Winners-PPt-and-Sources/SIH_2025_GeoGuards_High_Quality.pdf)
- **GeoGuards feasibility** is four crisp adjective-led claims (Time-feasible, Scalable, Low cost, Modular design). Each carries a one-line reason, e.g., "Time-feasible – dataset → training → dashboard", which uses an arrow chain as the argument. The "Challenges" section turns risk into **domain causes with countermeasures** (the pie chart) rather than generic project risks. — [GeoGuards p4](file:///Users/himanshusolanki/Downloads/sih/SIH-Winners-PPt-and-Sources/SIH_2025_GeoGuards_High_Quality.pdf)
- **GeoGuards impact** is stated in operator/business language: "Cuts downtime and losses from rockfalls", "Builds trust with regulators and stakeholders", "Shows AI as a cost-effective safety solution". It is then split Social / Economic / Environmental. The only numbers in the deck are the pie percentages (unsourced) and the dashboard axes. — [GeoGuards p4, p5](file:///Users/himanshusolanki/Downloads/sih/SIH-Winners-PPt-and-Sources/SIH_2025_GeoGuards_High_Quality.pdf)

### Inferences
- The shared winning logic is: **name the problem in the PS's own words → show a single tangible product → show you can build it (stack plus steps) → show who benefits**. Quantitative rigor was evidently not required at the idea-submission stage.
- GeoGuards's shift from "we will build" to "here is output from what we built" is the most important upgrade to copy.
- The generic risk bullets in Innovators ("Privacy concerns" → "Privacy protection") are a weakness a new deck can easily beat, for example by naming a concrete mechanism such as on-device face blurring or DPDP Act compliance.

### Gaps
- There is no evidence of what judges actually scored or said. The inferences about "why they won" rest on deck content alone.

## Q6. Cross-deck patterns and evolution from 2024 to 2025

### Takeaway
The skeleton stays the same (6-slide SIH template, blue footer, SIH bulb logo, team-name oval top-left, S/E/E impact split). The evolution from 2024 to 2025 is from **"describe the idea with boxes and stock images"** to **"prove the idea with a pipeline, data charts, real prototype screenshots and research figures"**, along with a cleaner white/outlined-card aesthetic.

### Cited Findings
- **Identical patterns across all three**
  - Same 6-slide order and same template chrome
  - The same purple-outlined team-name oval (probably copied from a shared template in circulation)
  - Title slide bullets in identical order with a blank Team ID
  - Impact framed by beneficiary and/or Social / Economic / Environmental
  - References as a bare link list
  - No quantitative KPIs (users, ₹ cost, accuracy, response time)

  — all three PDFs
- **Colored-box grids** are used as the main layout primitive in every deck: 3-up color blocks and 3-row matrix (Innovators), pastel pills and arrow bands (Techbyte), outlined shadow cards (GeoGuards). — all three PDFs
- **Diagram types by deck**: hub-and-spoke user flow (Innovators); radial mind map plus numbered zig-zag roadmap (Techbyte); vertical ML pipeline plus fan-out tree plus pie chart (GeoGuards). No deck has a proper layered system architecture (client/API/DB/ML tiers). — all three PDFs
- **2025 changes seen in GeoGuards**:
  - The footer text "@SIH Idea submission- Template" is gone, leaving just the blue bar and page number
  - Serif display titles
  - White cards with black outlines and hard shadows
  - Evidence visuals (prototype screenshots with timestamps, domain illustration, paper figures)
  - Explicit ML/AI vocabulary (YOLO, ML model, simulated datasets) in line with a 2025 PS that was itself titled "AI-Based..."
  - A numbered, labelled step pipeline (Step I–V)

  — [GeoGuards PDF](file:///Users/himanshusolanki/Downloads/sih/SIH-Winners-PPt-and-Sources/SIH_2025_GeoGuards_High_Quality.pdf) vs 2024 PDFs
- The SIH25071 PS drew many competing teams with similar AI/IoT approaches, so differentiation came through execution and presentation rather than the core idea. — [GitHub MineGuard-AI](https://github.com/dineshdinuu5219r-dot/MineGuard-AI); [YouTube SIH25071 The PRISM](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5m8iiJW8Ars)

### Inferences (design lessons for a new, unique deck style)
- **Keep the mandated 6-slide order**. Judges expect it, and all winners followed it.
- **Name the product on slide 2** (Techbyte, Innovators' WSI) and never leave placeholders (GeoGuards's "IDEA TITLE").
- **Show proof, not promises**: prototype screenshots with visible real-time data and a traffic-light output, as in GeoGuards.
- **Use one domain illustration** to set context (the GeoGuards mine cutaway) instead of generic stock "awareness" posters.
- **Use a cause→countermeasure map** (the GeoGuards pie plus callouts) as the feasibility/risk visual. It is more persuasive than generic risk bullets.
- **Room to beat all three**:
  - A real layered architecture diagram
  - Real tech logos
  - 2–3 sourced hard numbers (e.g., official accident or crime statistics)
  - Concrete, mechanism-level mitigations
  - Consistent typography
  - A filled references slide with authoritative citations
  - No typos or overflow

### Gaps
- Only one 2025 deck is in this set, so "evolution" is inferred from n=1 and may not generalise. Other 2025 decks in the folder (e.g., SIH_2025_Tech_Pioneers) were out of scope.
- Winner status of all three teams is unverified (see Q1 Gaps).
