# Turn Winning SIH Decks Into Evidence

The seven "winning" SIH decks in the local collection (2023–2025) all won on **fit to the problem statement plus one proof visual**, not on design. Every one is a white, Office-default slide set with serif caps headings, a blue footer bar and the SIH bulb logo. They carry typos, blank Team IDs, clipped diagrams and almost no sourced numbers. Only GeoGuards (2025) shows real prototype output, and only Tech Pioneers (2025) shows a competitor matrix. That leaves a wide-open lane. For ISRO's SIH26170 ("AI-Driven Anomaly Detection in Component Burn-In & Screening"), the recommended style is **"Screening Traveler"**, and the recommended product name is **PARAKH-168** (*parakh* is Hindi for assaying or testing true worth). In this style the six slides read as the sheets of a flight-part screening traveler. There is a gate rail across the top of each slide, a sheet block, read-point ticks at 0/24/96/168 h on every chart, reason-code chips in monospace, and three disposition shapes (■ REJECT, ▲ REVIEW, ● RELEASE-candidate) that carry meaning, not decoration. Each official slide borrows the strongest move from a winner and upgrades it with evidence:

- GeoGuards' output-as-proof becomes a two-part minimum demo on Slide 2.
- Nyay Sathi's colour-by-role architecture becomes a layered screening gate on Slide 3.
- GeoGuards' cause-to-countermeasure pie becomes a failure-mechanism-to-layer map on Slide 4.
- Cannon Crew's user story becomes a chamber-hour ledger on Slide 5.
- Tech Pioneers' tick matrix becomes an honest baseline comparison on Slide 6.

Two constraints override everything else. The **national upload window closes around 30 September 2026, three days from today**. And the **6-slide, PDF-only rule is confirmed only for 2025**; for 2026 it rests on secondary sources.

## Winners remove one constraint and prove it once

Under the surface noise, the decks share one line of reasoning. Each hook names **the constraint the user lives under, and the solution removes it**: language barriers (Nyay Sathi), no incentive to segregate waste (greenSort), no internet (Cannon Crew), no warning before a slope fails (GeoGuards). Each memorable element is a single-sentence promise paired with one proof visual. The strongest diagrams all tell a **closed-loop, end-to-end story**: input → processing → action → verification or feedback. Colour-coded role boxes (2023) read better at thumbnail size than monochrome Mermaid output (Cannon Crew) or pasted low-resolution figures (greenSort).

The decks also changed between 2024 and 2025. They moved from "describe the idea with boxes and stock images" (Innovators' posters, Techbyte's mind map) to "prove the idea with pipelines, data charts and screenshots" (GeoGuards). GeoGuards also dropped the "@SIH Idea submission- Template" footer text and adopted outlined hard-shadow cards with serif titles.

None of the seven has four things: a **layered architecture with an explicit ML node**, **sourced numbers**, **mechanism-level mitigations**, or a **dense, authoritative reference list**. The polish bar is also low. Spell-check underlines, clipped titles and blank Team IDs did not stop any of these decks. A coherent design system is therefore an immediate differentiator, but only if the content fits the problem statement first.

The other guides in the folder confirm the evaluation logic, and they conflict on format:

- **The TechDoodles 2026 Playbook** is third-party, despite its "OFFICIAL PLAYBOOK" badge. It lists "Weak problem understanding, vague impact metrics" as the national-round elimination cause and "Presenting only slides, no working demo" as the internal-round one. It adds that "a well-scoped, boring-sounding idea with a working model will consistently beat a flashy idea with mock screens". It also recommends a **9-slide** structure that cannot be submitted under a 6-slide cap ([Playbook](file:///Users/himanshusolanki/Downloads/sih/SIH-Winners-PPt-and-Sources/SIH_2026_Playbook_TechDoodles_Final.pdf)).
- **The README** recommends 10 slides ([README](file:///Users/himanshusolanki/Downloads/sih/SIH-Winners-PPt-and-Sources/README.md)).
- **The Easy-vs-Difficult guide** scores all 226 SIH 2026 problem statements on an unofficial keyword heuristic. It never mentions SIH26170. It does rate the closest analogue, SIH26057 (AI anomaly detection on sonar), as Difficult, and says of Difficult picks: "a far less crowded field and a much stronger wow factor" ([Easy vs Difficult](file:///Users/himanshusolanki/Downloads/sih/SIH-Winners-PPt-and-Sources/SIH%202026%20-%20Easy%20vs%20Difficult%20Problem%20Statements.pdf)).

Its per-problem schema (PROBLEM → WHY HARD → APPROACH → STACK → MVP) is a useful spine for slides 2–3.

## Six slides, PDF only, and a jury that asks about operations

The SIH 2025 template sets the rules:

- "Keep the maximum slides limit to six (6), including the title slide."
- "Save the file in PDF… No PPT, Word Doc or any other format will be supported."
- "Avoid paragraphs and post your idea in points/diagrams/Infographics/pictures."
- "Idea should be unique and novel."
- Teams must use the provided template "without changing the idea details pointers."

([Scribd: SIH2025 IDEA Presentation Format](https://www.scribd.com/document/906083636/SIH2025-IDEA-Presentation-Format)). The title slide must carry Problem Statement ID, Title, Theme, PS Category, Team ID and Team Name ([SlideShare template](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/sih2025-idea-presentation-format-pdf-its-just-a-format/283698583)). A 2026 winner's playbook still describes "exactly 6 slides" as a PDF, with the theme matching the portal ([Zaid Sayyed, SIH 2026 Playbook](https://zaidsayyed.in/blog/sih-2026)). **No 2026 template from sih.gov.in was verified.** Reskilll's claims of a "10 slides maximum", a 10 MB limit and a 14 pt minimum are the site's own advice ([Reskilll](https://reskilll.com/blogs/sih-2026-ppt-template-exact-format-slides-evaluators-score/)). Build to 6. A 6-slide deck is compliant under both readings; a 10-slide deck risks rejection under one.

The official criteria wording is "novelty of the idea, complexity, clarity and details in the prescribed format, feasibility, practicability, sustainability, scale of impact, user experience and potential for future work progression" ([thenewviews](https://thenewviews.com/win-smart-india-hackathon/)). Screeners reportedly spend 2–3 minutes per deck ([Reskilll](https://reskilll.com/blogs/sih-2026-ppt-template-exact-format-slides-evaluators-score/)). They check whether a team understood *the specific* statement and named a concrete stack instead of a vague "AI/ML layer". Finale juries ask about "operational ownership, policy compliance, cost-to-run calculations, field failure modes, and post-graduation maintenance plans rather than code sophistication" ([Zaid Sayyed](https://zaidsayyed.in/blog/sih-2026)).

SIH26170's own text sets what judges will check against ([SIH Buddy: SIH26170](https://www.sihbuddy.in/ps/SIH26170)):

- readings at 0/24/96/168 h;
- the 45 µA part in a 10 µA lot under a 50 µA limit;
- a 0 h + 24 h → 168 h drift regressor with QA explanations;
- a metric that "heavily penalizes false negatives";
- a minimum demo showing one in-spec lot outlier and one early drift flag, each with its reasoning;
- no supplied dataset.

**Competition is real.** At least nine public repos target SIH26170, including AETHER, SPAD, BurnGuard, VIGIL-X and Agni Pariksha ([AETHER](https://github.com/amol16112005/AETHER-SIH26170); [SPAD](https://github.com/mohith-reddy18/SIH26-SPAD); [BurnGuard](https://github.com/Chandrika1908/BurnGuard_AI_Demo)). At least two already ship the obvious "DPAT + Isolation Forest + LightGBM + SHAP + Streamlit" stack: space-grade-burnin-ai and ASTRA-IC, the latter claiming 100% recall on self-generated data ([space-grade-burnin-ai](https://github.com/aadityaexpress/space-grade-burnin-ai); [ASTRA-IC](https://github.com/jishasalariya/ASTRA-IC)). The deck therefore has to differentiate on **rigor made visible**, not on the stack.

On branding, ISRO permits free reproduction of isro.gov.in material with acknowledgement and without misleading context, but its policy is silent on the logo ([ISRO Copyright Policy](https://www.isro.gov.in/Copyright_Policy.html)). Write "Problem by: Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO)" as text. Do not co-brand with the ISRO logo, and keep only the SIH logo the template carries.

## The Screening Traveler style: the deck is a part passing through the gate

**Concept and rationale.** Space-grade parts move through screening with a paper trail: visual, temperature cycling, burn-in, pre- and post-burn-in electricals, then a PDA check against 5%. ISRO's own COTS screening matrix follows this sequence ([ISRO URSC COTS presentation](http://www.drsvsharma.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/USAGE-OF-COTS-EEE-PARTS-DrSVSharma-Deputy-Director-ISRO-URSC.pdf)). Screening Traveler styles each slide as one sheet of that traveler, so an ISRO reliability engineer recognises their own world in the layout before reading a word. It merges the two styles the rules-and-design notes rank highest. One is a light **"component datasheet"** base: ruled tables, small-caps labels, characteristic curves against burn-in hours. The other is a single dark **"telemetry panel"** used only for hero evidence on slides 2 and 3. The light base prints and projects safely, and the dark panel gives the one high-contrast focal point that 2026 "dark mode / data storytelling" trends reward ([SlideEgg 2026 trends](https://www.slideegg.com/blog/presentation-tips/the-7-presentation-design-trends-dominating-2026-data-backed/)). None of the seven winners, and none of the competitor repos, uses a domain-native visual language. Every one is Office blue on white.

**Template safety.** Build inside the official SIH PPT file. Keep its white background, the SIH logo at top-right, the blue footer bar (#0070C0) with the page number, and the exact six headings. All styling lives inside the content area. Nothing in the style needs the template chrome to change.

**Palette** (one neutral system; colour always means something; every status colour is paired with a shape):

| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Ink (navy) | `#0B1F3A` | Headings, primary text |
| Graphite | `#4A5568` | Body text, axis labels |
| Rule | `#D0D5DD` | Hairline rules, table borders, grid |
| Panel tint | `#F4F6F9` | Card and sheet backgrounds |
| Telemetry panel | `#0E1726` | Dark hero panel background (slides 2–3 only) |
| Trace cyan | `#4FD1E8` | Healthy-lot traces on the dark panel |
| Cryo blue (accent) | `#1F5FAD` | Links, the model/forecast line, "our layer" highlight |
| Reject red ■ | `#C8102E` | REJECT, spec lines, escapes |
| Review amber ▲ | `#E8A200` | REVIEW, PAT fences, conformal band edge (fills only, never text) |
| Release green ● | `#00876C` | RELEASE-candidate, "met" ticks |
| SIH saffron / green | `#F58220` / `#2E8B3A` | Logo area only, never in data |

**Fonts** (free on Google Fonts; embed them when exporting the PDF):

- **IBM Plex Sans Condensed SemiBold** for headings: 32–36 pt titles, 20 pt section labels in small caps with +6% tracking.
- **IBM Plex Sans** for body text at ≥14 pt.
- **IBM Plex Mono** for every number, part ID, reason code, equation and the sheet block.

One family, three cuts. This avoids the three-to-four-font mixing seen in Techbyte and Tech Pioneers.

**Grid.** Use a 16:9 canvas at 1920×1080, a 12-column grid with 72 px outer margins and 24 px gutters, and a 4 px baseline. The layout runs top to bottom as follows:

| Band | y (px) | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| Template header | 0–140 | Team badge top-left; official heading; SIH logo top-right |
| Gate rail | 150–190 | The rail motif (below) |
| Content | 200–1000 | Slide content |
| Template footer | 1024–1080 | Footer bar and page number |
| Sheet block | above the footer, right | Sheet block motif (below) |

Keep one visual claim per slide that can be read in about 20 seconds, and no more than about 60 words of body copy outside diagrams.

**Iconography.** Use Lucide or Phosphor line icons at a 2 px stroke in Ink. No clip-art, no emoji and no stock photos. Draw three custom glyphs from rectangles and lines and reuse them: a **burn-in chamber** (box with a door and heat wave), a **DUT board** (rectangle with a 4×6 socket grid), and a **lot tray** (rectangle with a 6×8 dot matrix, one dot red).

**Visual motifs.** These five devices repeat on every slide:

1. **Gate rail.** A thin 2 px Rule-coloured line across the grid, carrying six stations: `01 INTAKE · 02 CLAIM · 03 GATE · 04 QUALIFY · 05 RELEASE · 06 TRACE`. These map to the six template slots. The current station is a filled Ink circle with a mono label; past stations are hollow.
2. **Sheet block.** A ruled box at bottom-right, 360×56 px, in Plex Mono 11 pt: `SIH26170 · PARAKH-168 · SHEET 2/6 · REV A · TEAM <ID>`. It borrows the datasheet revision block.
3. **Read-point ticks.** Every time-axis in the deck uses only 0 · 24 · 96 · 168 h, with 24 h marked "decision point". This makes the problem's four read-points the deck's rhythm.
4. **Reason-code chips.** Rounded rectangles in Plex Mono 12 pt with a 1 px border, e.g. `R01 +6.0σ vs lot`. The explainability promise becomes a visible visual unit.
5. **Disposition shapes.** ■ REJECT (red), ▲ REVIEW (amber), ◆ CONTINUE (cryo blue), ● RELEASE-candidate (green). Status is never shown by colour alone.

## Slide-by-slide blueprint for the SIH26170 idea PDF

Technical content comes from the earlier burn-in research. The screening regime is MIL-STD-883 Method 1015: minimum 160 h at 125 °C for Class B and 240 h for Class S ([JPL](https://parts.jpl.nasa.gov/asic/Sect.4.3.html)). ESCC 9000 drift rules measure "related to the initial measurement", with a lot PDA of 5% ([ESCC 9000](https://escies.org/download/specdraftapppub?id=3659)). Lot-relative screening follows AEC-Q001 robust PAT: median ± k·IQR/1.35, with PAT never loosening spec ([AEC-Q001 Rev D](http://www.aecouncil.com/Documents/AEC_Q001_Rev_D.pdf)).

**Heading:** keep the template title block exactly. Fill every field and leave nothing blank:

- Problem Statement ID – SIH26170
- Problem Statement Title – AI-Driven Anomaly Detection in Component Burn-In & Screening
- Theme – Smart Automation
- PS Category – Software
- Team ID – (fill from the portal)
- Team Name – (as registered)

Check the Theme string against the portal; the rules require an exact match. Below the fields, add one text line: "Problem by: Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO)".

**Key lines:** product lockup "PARAKH-168" in Plex Sans Condensed SemiBold 40 pt Ink, with the tagline in Plex Sans 18 pt Graphite: *"Catch the in-spec outlier at hour 0. Call the drift at hour 24. Explain every flag."*

**Diagram (right half, replacing the template's hexagon art only if the template allows; otherwise place it bottom-right):** a **lot tray**, 520×380 px. Draw an 8×6 grid of 28 px circles in Panel-tint fill with Rule outlines. One circle, row 3 column 6, is filled Reject red and joined by a 1.5 px leader line to a chip reading `P-0317 · IDDQ 45 µA · spec ≤ 50 µA · lot median 10 µA · R01 +6.0σ`. Under the tray, draw a mini read-point axis with ticks 0 · 24 · 96 · 168 h and 24 h circled. The meaning: one part in 48 is in spec and still wrong, which is the problem statement's own example, shown before a word of explanation.

**Borrows and improves:** Techbyte's and Innovators' product naming. It fixes the blank Team ID (Innovators, GeoGuards, Tech Pioneers) and Tech Pioneers' logo collision.

### Slide 2: Idea Title / Proposed Solution (gate station 02 CLAIM)

**Heading:** "PARAKH-168: a recall-first screening gate that explains every flag"

**Key lines:** use three columns whose headers copy the template sub-prompts word for word, the Cannon Crew device. Each column holds three lines of at most 12 words.

- *Detailed explanation.*
  - Module A: lot-relative outlier screen (Dynamic PAT on log values + drift features).
  - Module B: 24 h → 168 h drift forecast with a calibrated upper bound.
  - Every flag carries a deterministic reason code and a counterfactual.
- *How it addresses the problem.*
  - Flags parts that pass the datasheet but break from their own lot.
  - Rejects or releases at 24 h only when the bound is statistically safe.
  - Is tuned for zero false negatives on held-out lots.
- *Innovation and uniqueness.*
  - The flag fires on the **conformal upper bound**, not the point estimate.
  - Physics-labelled reason codes (BTI-like drift, oxide-breakdown precursor).
  - An escape-rate bound (≤ 3/N) instead of a bare "100% recall".

**Diagram (hero, dark telemetry panel, full width, 1776×420 px, below the columns): "Two parts, two reasons."** Split the panel into two charts.

*Left chart: Part A, the in-spec outlier.* Show a horizontal strip plot of log Iddq for a 48-part lot at 24 h:

- 47 Trace-cyan dots jittered around 10 µA.
- A dashed amber vertical line labelled "PAT fence · median + 4σ_robust (log)".
- A solid red vertical line labelled "spec 50 µA".
- One red ■ dot at 45 µA, between the two lines.
- Callout card: `■ REJECT · R01 Iddq@24h +6.0σ above lot median (n=48) · would pass if ≤ 16 µA`.

Replace these numbers with the prototype's real output.

*Right chart: Part B, early drift.* Show a trajectory chart:

- x-axis ticks 0 / 24 / 96 / 168 h; y-axis leakage in µA.
- A pale cyan band for the lot P5–P95 fan.
- Solid white observed points at 0 h and 24 h.
- A dashed cryo-blue median forecast to 168 h.
- An amber-edged conformal band (90%) widening to 168 h.
- A red horizontal "safety limit" line, crossed by the **upper band edge** while the median stays below it.
- Callout: `▲ REVIEW at 24h · R07 predicted I@168h = 41 µA [90% PI 37–45] > safety limit`.

A footer strip in mono 12 pt reads: `Output from PARAKH-168 prototype on physics-injected synthetic lots`.

**Borrows and improves:** GeoGuards' DATA → Low/Medium/High fan-out into real screenshots, the strongest element in the collection. Here the proof is the problem statement's exact minimum demo, with the reasoning printed on the output. Export these charts from the real prototype (Matplotlib/Plotly to SVG), not as mockups.

### Slide 3: Technical Approach (gate station 03 GATE)

**Heading:** "Technical Approach: a layered, OR-gated screen from datasheet limits to conformal forecasts"

**Key lines** (short mono labels on the diagram, not bullets):

- Gate 0 never loosens the spec.
- Every layer is conformal-calibrated on known-good parts: P(flag | good) ≤ α per layer.
- Validation uses GroupKFold by lot, with lot statistics computed inside each fold.

**Diagram (hero, 1776×520 px): "The screening gate."** A left-to-right pipeline on a light background, drawn in draw.io or Figma. Box fill encodes role, following Nyay Sathi's colour-by-role method:

- data = Panel tint with an Ink border;
- deterministic rules = white with a 2 px Reject-red border;
- statistics = white with an amber border;
- ML = white with a cryo-blue border;
- decision/human = Ink fill with white text.

Nodes, each 200×120 px, joined by 2 px arrows. Put the data shape in mono 11 pt on each arrow.

1. **Physics-injected generator.** Inset stacked bar of the defect mix: latent drift 40% · step 20% · in-spec outlier 25% · intermittent 10% · hard fail 5%. Arrow: `lots × parts × {Iddq, leak, delay} × {0,24,96,168}`.
2. **Ingest + schema check.** Unit normalisation, dedupe. Arrow: `tidy long table`.
3. **Gate 0: spec + Δ limits.** Show `Δ = max(Δabs, k·|V0|)` and the lot PDA tally.
4. **A1: Dynamic PAT.** Show `z = (x − median)/(IQR/1.35)` on log values, Δ, slope, curvature and residual-to-lot-median, with k ≈ 3.5–4.5 one-sided.
5. **A2: Robust Mahalanobis.** MCD fit, χ² threshold. Mini ellipse glyph.
6. **A3: IForest + ECOD.** PyOD ensemble with a golden-lot static PAT backstop.
7. **B: Drift forecast.** Show `V̂168 = V0 + D24·7ⁿ`, then "L1 LightGBM on Δ target, median-blend", then "CQR band (MAPIE)". Flag rule: `(U168 − V0)/168 > S or U168 > limit`.

Draw nodes 3–6 stacked in a bracket labelled "Module A", with node 7 parallel beneath it. All five feed a large **OR** node, which leads to four disposition chips (■ REJECT · ▲ REVIEW · ◆ CONTINUE · ● RELEASE-candidate). The chips lead to a final **QA / MRB console** node (Ink fill), whose small print reads "reason codes · counterfactual · TreeSHAP · append-only audit log". Draw a dashed return arrow from the console back to the generator labelled "inspector overrides → recalibration".

**Stack strip** (bottom 120 px, grouped logos in three labelled bins, about 10 logos total rather than Tech Pioneers' 45):

- *Data*: Python, pandas.
- *Models*: scikit-learn, PyOD, LightGBM, MAPIE, SHAP.
- *Delivery*: FastAPI, Streamlit, Docker (on-prem).

**Borrows and improves:** Cannon Crew's closed pipeline ending in verification, and Nyay Sathi's role colours. It also fixes the gap in the whole collection by giving the ML stages explicit, named nodes with their equations.

### Do and don't checklist

**Do:**
- Build inside the official template and keep its six headings verbatim.
- Name the product on slides 1–2.
- Show the problem statement's two-part minimum demo as real prototype output.
- Draw the ML stages as named nodes with their equations.
- Pair every risk with a mechanism.
- Use colour only with a shape.
- Put numbers in mono.
- Print full reference URLs.
- Write "Problem by ISRO" in text.
- Export charts as vectors.
- Embed fonts.
- Keep each slide's body copy near 60 words.

**Don't:**
- Exceed 6 slides.
- Upload PPTX.
- Leave "IDEA TITLE", "TO BE UPDATED" or a blank Team ID.
- Paste low-resolution screenshots of text.
- List 45 technologies.
- Claim "100% recall" or "0% escape" without the held-out-lot N and the 3/N bound.
- Cite unsourced percentages.
- Use the ISRO logo or State Emblem.
- Use stock posters or emoji in titles.
- Use Excalidraw hand-drawn diagrams, which clash with the datasheet style.
- Let spell-check squiggles into the export.

## Conclusion

The collection's real lesson is that SIH screening rewards **evidence density per slide**, and the bar is low: no winner combined real output, named ML mechanics, sourced numbers and mechanism-level risks. For SIH26170 the field is crowded with near-identical stacks, so design only helps when it *carries* rigor. The Screening Traveler grammar does that. The reason-code chips, the read-point axis and the disposition shapes each turn a technical differentiator (conformal upper-bound flagging, lot-relative robust statistics, escape bounds) into something a screener absorbs in 20 seconds. The deck then speaks the ISRO reliability engineer's language before the ML engineer's.

Two uncertainties remain for the team to resolve directly. The first is the 2026 template's exact rules and whether a video is required. The second is the definition ISRO intends for the "safety slope", which can move the Module B flag several-fold and should be stated on Slide 3 rather than buried. With three days left, the highest-return work is to render the Part A / Part B charts from the running prototype first. They are the one element no public winner or competitor has, and every other slide can lean on them.
