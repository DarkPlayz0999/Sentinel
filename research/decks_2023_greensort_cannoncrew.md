## Q1. Slide structure and order: headings and key lines, quoted

### Takeaway
Both 2024 decks follow the official SIH 2024 six-slide idea template exactly: Title, Idea Title, Technical Approach, Feasibility & Viability, Impact & Benefits, Research & References. The 2023 deck uses the older 2023 layout: Team Details & Problem Statement, Idea & Approach, Use Case Diagram, Tech Stack + Process Flow, Additional Features, Team Members. It has no explicit feasibility, risk, impact, or references slide. Each of these decks wins on one sharp idea plus one diagram, not on breaking the template.

### Cited Findings

**Official template (context)**
- The SIH 2024 idea template allows at most 6 slides including the title. The fixed order is: Title (PS ID, PS Title, Theme, PS Category, Team ID, Team Name), Idea Title (detailed solution, how it addresses the problem, innovation & uniqueness), Technical Approach (technologies, methodology, flow charts/images/prototype), Feasibility & Viability (feasibility, challenges & risks, strategies), Impact & Benefits, Research & References. It instructs teams to "avoid paragraphs" and use points, diagrams, and infographics, and submissions must be PDF only — [Scribd: SIH 2024 Idea Submission Template](https://www.scribd.com/presentation/831331764/SIH2024-IDEA-Presentation-Format-Ss)
- The Cannon Crew deck appears publicly as "SIH-2024-Winning-PPT" — [SlideShare](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/906186982-sih-2024-winning-ppt-pptx-sih-winning/283925301). ## Q3. The thinking: problem framing, hook, novelty/USP, feasibility, risks, impact, numbers

### Takeaway
Each winner has one crisp differentiator that fits in a headline:
- 2023: "legal help in plain, native language + blockchain-secured evidence"
- GreenSort: "classify waste by photo and get paid in cashback/crypto"
- Cannon Crew: "AI that works with zero internet" (air-gapped, local LLM)

Quantitative evidence is almost absent. The only numbers are "2000+ images", "100% offline", and "1860" (IPC year).

### Cited Findings
- **2023 framing/hook**: a social-justice framing. "limited legal awareness... affecting marginalized communities", with root causes named: "language barriers" and "the intricate nature of the legal system". The USP bundle is: multilingual (Hindi, Marathi, Urdu), "Know Your Rights" framework, case file summarizer, blockchain/IPFS storage, WebAuthn biometric, lawyer matchmaking, and gamified legal education. There is no feasibility, risk, or impact slide, and no numbers — [2023 deck p.2, p.4, p.5](file:///Users/himanshusolanki/Downloads/sih/SIH-Winners-PPt-and-Sources/SIH_2023_Presentation_Clean.pdf)
- **GreenSort framing**: a community-platform framing ("connects community, local authorities, and businesses"). The tagline hook is "A Cleaner future starts with us". Novelty comes from incentives (cashback, coupons, "cryptocurrency and NFTs") plus a recycled-product marketplace ("Recycle Mart") and route optimisation.
  - Feasibility is split into technical, operational, and economic. The revenue model: "charging a minimal fee from vendors" + e-commerce.
  - Risks are set out as three challenge→solution pairs with ➢/❖ bullets.
  - Impact is a five-dimension Impact/Benefit matrix with social (sanitation, engagement), economic (costs), and data-governance angles.
  - Only number: "2000+ pre-trained images" (used twice).
  - The problem statement is "Student Innovation" (an open PS), so the team had to self-define the problem. — [GreenSort deck p.1–p.5](file:///Users/himanshusolanki/Downloads/sih/SIH-Winners-PPt-and-Sources/SIH_2024_AKY_GreenSort_AI.pdf)
- **Cannon Crew framing**: a security/compliance framing for government. The novelty is the air-gap constraint: "Runs locally... without internet. Even the AI component."
  - Feasibility argument: "Proven scripting techniques and tools like Ollama allow for full remote management and 100% offline functionality"; "requires less computing power".
  - Risks are honest and specific: "Administrative Access Required", "Integration Complexity with diverse and potentially outdated systems". Mitigations: RBAC, a testing suite, and a configuration database.
  - Impact is made concrete through a **user story** (a government agency on air-gapped machines), the only narrative scenario in the three decks.
  - Numbers: "100%" offline (twice). — [Cannon Crew deck p.2–p.5](file:///Users/himanshusolanki/Downloads/sih/SIH-Winners-PPt-and-Sources/SIH_2024_Cannon_Crew_High_Quality.pdf)
- Background on the problem: CIS publishes benchmark-based GPO templates and build kits, so automated CIS hardening is a real enterprise workflow. That supports Cannon Crew's feasibility claim that the target artefacts exist — [CIS SecureSuite Build Kit](https://www.cisecurity.org/cis-securesuite/cis-securesuite-build-kit-content); [Berkeley ISO: CIS GPO templates](https://security.berkeley.edu/news/new-cis-benchmark-based-gpo-templates-now-available)

### Inferences
- The hook is always the constraint the user lives under: language/complexity (2023), no incentive to segregate waste (GreenSort), no internet (Cannon Crew). Designing the pitch around "the constraint we remove" is a repeatable pattern.
- Cannon Crew's pairing of challenge and mitigation on the same slide, plus a persona/user story, is the most judge-friendly reasoning. GreenSort's risk mitigations are generic ("Develop detailed budgets with contingency plans").
- GreenSort overloads buzzwords (IoT, crypto, NFTs, Django alongside Node). Judges still selected it, which suggests breadth and the incentive idea outweighed rigour at screening. This is an inference, not confirmed.

### Gaps
- No deck shows market size, cost estimates, accuracy metrics, or user research numbers. I found no source on how judges scored these specific decks.

## Q4. Visual design: palette, typography, density, logos, whitespace, consistency

### Takeaway
All three are plain white-background, template-driven decks with low design ambition. Their visual identity comes from the SIH orange/green logo and a single blue accent. None use a custom brand palette, a dark theme, or photography. Text density is high in the 2024 decks (about 150–250 words on the busiest slides) despite the template's "avoid paragraphs" rule.

### Cited Findings
- **2023 palette/type**: white background. Headings are a serif (Georgia/DejaVu-Serif-like) in pure saturated blue, sampled around #0000FD / #010CFB. Table labels are bold blue sans (Arial) on white. ## Q6. Cross-deck patterns

### Takeaway
Winning SIH idea decks share the same things:
- strict template compliance (6 slides)
- a named product with a tagline or constraint-based title
- one end-to-end flowchart on the Technical Approach slide
- a logo-wall tech stack
- bold lead-in bullets ("Keyword: explanation")
- risk→mitigation pairs
- Impact/Benefit split
- references as links

They do not share design polish or data/metrics. Those are the open space for a distinctive new style.

### Cited Findings
- All three use a white background with a blue accent (#0000FD in 2023; #0070C0/#0171C1 footer bars in 2024) and the SIH orange/green logo — [2023](file:///Users/himanshusolanki/Downloads/sih/SIH-Winners-PPt-and-Sources/SIH_2023_Presentation_Clean.pdf), [GreenSort](file:///Users/himanshusolanki/Downloads/sih/SIH-Winners-PPt-and-Sources/SIH_2024_AKY_GreenSort_AI.pdf), [Cannon Crew](file:///Users/himanshusolanki/Downloads/sih/SIH-Winners-PPt-and-Sources/SIH_2024_Cannon_Crew_High_Quality.pdf)
- All three give a boxed-arrow flowchart the largest visual area on slide 3 or 4. All three show a tech stack as brand logos. Both 2024 decks put a team-name oval top-left and the SIH logo top-right on every content slide — same sources
- Both 2024 decks structure feasibility as feasibility → challenges → strategies, mirroring the template prompts exactly — [Scribd template](https://www.scribd.com/presentation/831331764/SIH2024-IDEA-Presentation-Format-Ss)
- Bold lead-in bullet pattern ("**Automation:** Turn CIS guidelines into PowerShell"; "**Technical Feasibility:** Utilizes reliable technologies") appears in both 2024 decks. The 2023 deck uses bold key phrases inside prose — same deck sources
- None include quantified market/impact data, cost, a timeline/roadmap with dates, or team credentials. Only Cannon Crew uses a persona/user story. Only 2023 shows a UI mockup — same deck sources

### Inferences (design opportunities for a new unique style)
- Keep: exact template headings and order, a named product + tagline, one closed-loop architecture diagram, logo-stack, bold-lead bullets, risk↔mitigation pairing, and a user-story scenario.
- Beat the baseline:
  - Replace text walls with 3-column rubric cards (Cannon Crew style) holding 12–15 words per bullet.
  - Colour-code architecture layers by role (2023 style) instead of Mermaid defaults.
  - Add one UI mockup (2023 style).
  - Add 2–3 hard numbers (problem scale, model accuracy, cost/time saved), which none of the winners had.
  - Make the Impact slide a before/after or persona journey.
  - Print actual URLs/citations.
  - Export at high resolution with spell-check off.
- A custom but restrained palette would differentiate instantly without looking off-template, for example keeping the SIH blue footer but adding a domain accent colour, as all three stuck to Office defaults.

### Gaps
- Three decks is a small sample. The patterns should be cross-checked against the other decks in the folder (Innovators, Techbyte, GeoGuards, Tech Pioneers, which were not in this assignment's scope).
