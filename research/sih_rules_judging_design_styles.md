# SIH 2025/2026 Idea-PPT Rules, Judging Criteria, Winning-Deck Traits, and Distinctive Deck Design Styles (for ISRO SIH26170)

Research date: 2026-09-27. Scope: official template rules, screening and finale judging, winner and mentor tips, public winning decks, modern technical-deck styles, and how to use ISRO context safely.

## 1. Official SIH idea-presentation template: slides, sections, format, size, identity rules, submission process, internal rubric

### Takeaway
The official SIH 2025 template is 6 slides maximum, including the title slide. It must be uploaded as a PDF only, and teams must keep the template's section pointers unchanged. The slides are: Title, Idea/Proposed Solution, Technical Approach, Feasibility & Viability, Impact & Benefits, and Research & References. Nothing I found shows an official change for 2026: the main 2026 guides and a local winner deck still use the same 6-slide structure. Team names are not anonymized. The title slide asks for Team ID and Team Name.

### Cited Findings
- The official SIH2025 template says: "Keep the maximum slides limit to six (6), including the title slide"; "save the file in PDF and upload the same on portal. No PPT, Word Doc or any other format will be supported"; "avoid paragraphs and post your idea in points/diagrams/Infographics/pictures"; "Idea should be unique and novel"; and teams "can only use provided template ... without changing the idea details pointers." — [Scribd: SIH2025 IDEA Presentation Format](https://www.scribd.com/document/906083636/SIH2025-IDEA-Presentation-Format); [SlideShare copy of template](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/sih2025-idea-presentation-format-pdf-its-just-a-format/283698583)
- Official slide-by-slide pointers (SIH 2025) — [SlideShare copy of template](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/sih2025-idea-presentation-format-pdf-its-just-a-format/283698583):
  1. **Title:** Problem Statement ID, Problem Statement Title, Theme, PS Category (Software/Hardware), Team ID, Team Name (as registered on the portal).
  2. **Idea Title / Proposed Solution:** a detailed explanation of the solution, how it addresses the problem, and its innovation and uniqueness.
  3. **Technical Approach:** technologies (programming languages, frameworks, hardware), plus the methodology and implementation process ("Flow Charts/Images/working prototype").
  4. **Feasibility and Viability:** a feasibility analysis, potential challenges and risks, and strategies for overcoming them.
  5. **Impact and Benefits:** the potential impact on the target audience, plus social, economic and environmental benefits.
  6. **Research and References:** details and links for references and research work.
- The 2026 winner's playbook says the deck is "Exactly 6 slides": title, proposed solution, technical approach, feasibility/viability, impact/benefits, research/references, submitted as a PDF. It says the theme must match the portal exactly. — [Zaid Sayyed, SIH 2026 Winner's Playbook](https://zaidsayyed.in/blog/sih-2026)

### Inferences
- Build to the official 6-slide PDF. A 10-slide deck risks disqualification or failing screening. Keep the exact official section headings as anchors, because evaluators check them against the template.
- Since evaluators may spend only 2–3 minutes per deck, each slide should carry one clear visual claim a reader can take in within about 20 seconds.
- Keep the PDF lean (for example, under 10MB with images embedded at around 150–200 dpi). No official limit was found, but portals often cap uploads.
- A 90–120 second demo video of the burn-in dashboard is worth preparing in case the 2026 portal asks for one.

### Gaps
- I could not open the official 2026 template PDF or portal guideline PDF on sih.gov.in. The claim that the 2026 template is identical to 2025 rests on secondary sources and on SIH26170 decks by other teams on GitHub.
- There is no official file-size limit and no official scoring weights. No official rule on college logos or identity was found either: the template itself asks for Team Name, and some colleges add their own logo, which is neither shown as allowed nor prohibited.
- I could not verify the 2026 video-demo requirement (length, hosting) on sih.gov.in.

## 2. Judging criteria at screening and Grand Finale, and what jury members value

### Takeaway
The official SIH criteria language is: "novelty of the idea, complexity, clarity and details in the prescribed format, feasibility, practicability, sustainability, scale of impact, user experience and potential for future work progression". Screening rewards being specific to the exact problem statement: named components, real constraints, and each risk paired with a mitigation. Finale juries (ministry and industry) ask about deployment: who operates it, cost to run, failure modes, and maintenance.

### Cited Findings
- SIH evaluation criteria: "novelty of the idea, complexity, clarity and details in the prescribed format, feasibility, practicability, sustainability, scale of impact, user experience and potential for future work progression." — search summary citing [sih.gov.in](https://sih.gov.in/) and [thenewviews](https://thenewviews.com/win-smart-india-hackathon/)
- In screening, evaluators check whether a team understood the *specific* statement or is offering a generic solution. They look for a named tech stack rather than a vague "AI/ML layer", and for feasibility that acknowledges real constraints. — [Zaid Sayyed](https://zaidsayyed.in/blog/sih-2026)
- Finale jury questions (the playbook documents 26) focus on "operational ownership, policy compliance, cost-to-run calculations, field failure modes, and post-graduation maintenance plans rather than code sophistication." — [Zaid Sayyed](https://zaidsayyed.in/blog/sih-2026)
- Traits of winning decks, per the same playbook:
  - Each risk is paired with a concrete mitigation.
  - "What if" failure modes are shown pre-emptively.
  - Viability is split into technical, economic and operational parts.
  - References name real sources.
  - Winners "answer the jury before the jury speaks" and name their weaknesses.

  — [Zaid Sayyed](https://zaidsayyed.in/blog/sih-2026)
- Common losing mistakes:
  - Template placeholder text left in (e.g., "TO BE UPDATED").
  - Mismatched theme.
  - Duplicated slides.
  - Visible draft notes.
  - Demo links on free hosts that go to sleep.

  — [Zaid Sayyed](https://zaidsayyed.in/blog/sih-2026)
- A winner's LinkedIn post says:
  - "Keep slides clean, consistent & professional, clutter kills."
  - "No long paragraphs, clarity > quantity."
  - "Show prototype screenshots or demo video, execution > promises."
  - Cover the What/Why/How.
  - Quantify who benefits and by how much.
  - Mistakes to avoid: "Slides overloaded with theory (Judges don't want a textbook)" and "No prototype in early rounds (Even a rough demo beats theory)."

  — [Jatin Vishwakarma, LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/jatin-vishwakarma-563914246_sih2025-smartindiahackathon-hackathon-activity-7366335053787709440-kMce)
- A two-time winner's guide (2022/2023) advises:
  - Validate the problem with stakeholders.
  - Be ready to justify every technical choice (e.g., why this database).
  - Add a Business Model Canvas or competitor analysis when the judges are business-minded.
  - Be candid about pivots and about plans you couldn't execute.
  - Everyone on the team should be able to present.
  - Mistakes: misreading the problem statement, having no stakeholder validation, and sending LinkedIn requests to judges during the event.

  — [Ishika Jain, DEV](https://dev.to/ishikajain/a-beginners-guide-to-winning-smart-india-hackathon-1pei)
- SIH26170 specifics that judges will check against (problem statement text) — [SIH Buddy: SIH26170](https://www.sihbuddy.in/ps/SIH26170):
  - Parametric readings at 0, 24, 96 and 168 hours.
  - Module 1: a dynamic lot-relative outlier detector. Example: a 45µA part in a 10µA-average lot, still within the 50µA limit.
  - Module 2: a drift regressor that predicts 168-hour values from 0- and 24-hour readings, with actionable QA explanations.
  - The evaluation metric "heavily penalizes false negatives".
  - The minimum viable demo shows two parts: one that passes limits but is flagged as a lot outlier, and one flagged early by drift prediction, each with reasoning.
  - No dataset is provided, so teams must synthesize realistic data with embedded latent defects.
- Theme / category: ISRO, Smart Automation, Software. — [SIH Buddy](https://www.sihbuddy.in/ps/SIH26170)
- A figure circulating in other teams' SIH26170 material: "85.7% of chamber time is spent on parts that are fine" (i.e., 144 of 168 h if a part could be released at 24 h). This is a team-derived framing, not an ISRO statistic. — search summary of [GitHub SIH26170 repos](https://github.com/amol16112005/AETHER-SIH26170)
- Competition: at least 9 public GitHub repos already target SIH26170 (AETHER, SPAD, BurnGuard, VIGIL-X, Agni Pariksha, etc.). — [GitHub search results, e.g. AETHER](https://github.com/amol16112005/AETHER-SIH26170), [SPAD](https://github.com/mohith-reddy18/SIH26-SPAD), [BurnGuard](https://github.com/Chandrika1908/BurnGuard_AI_Demo)

### Inferences
- Map the rubric onto the 6 slides for SIH26170:
  - **Novelty:** the pairing of lot-relative outlier detection with early drift forecasting.
  - **Complexity:** the synthetic data generator with injected latent-defect physics.
  - **Clarity:** the two-part minimum-demo story.
  - **Feasibility:** explicitly tuning for false negatives, i.e., showing recall/cost-weighted thresholds.
  - **UX:** the QA-inspector explanation card.
  - **Future work:** real ISRO lot data, and extending to other parameters and screening stages.
- Make the minimum viable demo literally visible on the Proposed Solution slide. Show two annotated part traces: "Part A: in spec, but 4.5× lot median" and "Part B: predicted to breach at 168 h from its 24 h reading". This answers the problem statement in its own terms.
- Many teams share this problem and use similar names and stacks. Specificity and false-negative-aware evaluation will separate decks more than generic "AI dashboard" claims. Likely jury questions: who acts on a flag, what the false-alarm cost is, how the tool integrates with existing ESS records, and whether it runs on-premise or air-gapped (ISRO data sensitivity).

### Gaps
- I found no named, attributable ISRO or ministry jury quotes about what they value. Finale-jury insights come from winners' secondary accounts.
- Official criterion weights were not found.

## 3. Publicly shared, highly regarded SIH PPTs and their visual devices

### Takeaway
Publicly shared winning decks, such as the local repo copy of SIH 2025 winner "GeoGuards", are visually plain. They use a white background, the SIH logo top-right, serif all-caps section headings, a blue footer bar with page number, rounded bullet boxes, stock or process illustrations, flowcharts and pie charts. Winners won on content fit, not design, so there is a lot of room to stand out visually while still keeping the template's section structure.

### Cited Findings
- The local repo `/Users/himanshusolanki/Downloads/sih/SIH-Winners-PPt-and-Sources` (a clone of [Aadiii00/SIH-Winners-PPt-and-Sources](https://github.com/Aadiii00/SIH-Winners-PPt-and-Sources)) holds winner decks: SIH 2023; SIH 2024 AKY GreenSort AI, Cannon Crew, Innovators, Techbyte; SIH 2025 GeoGuards and Tech Pioneers; and a 2026 playbook PDF. Each is 1.1–2.2 MB. — local files (listed via shell)
- Observed in the SIH 2025 GeoGuards deck (PS SIH25071, AI rockfall prediction; read directly, `SIH_2025_GeoGuards_High_Quality.pdf`):
  - Exactly 6 slides following the official headings.
  - Slide 1 lists all title fields, including Team Name, over the SIH bulb-brain logo.
  - A team-name oval sits top-left on each slide.
  - Slide 2 uses a 3-column layout: "Addressing the Problem" box, a central annotated mine cross-section illustration, and "Solution overview" cards.
  - Slide 3 uses a colored 5-step vertical flowchart plus three risk-level output chart screenshots (low, medium, high) as a "prototype preview".
  - Slide 4 uses feasibility cards (time, scale, cost, modular) plus a pie chart of risk causes with callouts.
  - Slide 5 has an impacts box, Social/Economic/Environmental bubbles, and research figures.
  - Slide 6 has sparse references (one MDPI link).

  — [Aadiii00 repo](https://github.com/Aadiii00/SIH-Winners-PPt-and-Sources) (local copy)
- The same winner-deck collections are reposted on SlideShare and Scribd, e.g. ["smart india hackathon winning ppt for the 2026"](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/smart-india-hackothaon-winning-ppt-for-the-2026/284297693) and [SIH 2025 PPT Guide](https://www.scribd.com/document/899483721/Smart-India-Hackathon-SIH-PPT-Round-a-Complete-Guide-1)
- A GitHub markdown template for SIH 2026 exists: [tharsan1305/SIH-2026](https://github.com/tharsan1305/SIH-2026/blob/main/SIH2026_PPT_Submission_Template.md). A YouTube walkthrough of the "SIH 2026 Official PPT Template ... 6 Slides" is also available ([BlinkNBuild](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R9vTqoAR5pg)); its video title supports the 6-slide count for 2026.

### Inferences
- The typical winning deck is a "template + boxes + flowchart + screenshot" deck. A deck that keeps the six headings and the official SIH logo, then adds a coherent visual system (below), would stand out clearly in screening.
- Devices worth keeping from the winners: prototype screenshots on the Technical Approach slide, a structured Social/Economic/Environmental impact block, and a feasibility slide built from cards. The reference slide is where winners are weak. A dense, numbered, standards-based reference list is an easy differentiator.

### Gaps
- Only one winner deck was inspected in detail (GeoGuards). The others in the local repo were not opened because of the tool budget; another researcher may cover them.

## 4. Distinctive modern design styles for a space and deep-tech deck: diagrams, explainability visuals, typography, color, tools

### Takeaway
Current design trends are bento grids, dark mode, bold oversized headlines, and "data storytelling" with stripped-down, high-contrast charts. They map naturally onto a "mission control / telemetry" or "component datasheet" look for SIH26170.

### Cited Findings
- 2026 presentation trends:
  - Bento grid: compartmentalized rounded blocks that keep one slide scannable while showing several items.
  - Dark mode, which signals "tech-forward, premium".
  - Oversized bold sans-serif headlines.
  - Moving from "data dumping" to "data storytelling" with simplified, high-contrast charts that strip grid lines and unnecessary axes.
  - Kinetic typography, which is irrelevant for a static PDF.

  — [SlideEgg: 7 Presentation Design Trends 2026](https://www.slideegg.com/blog/presentation-tips/the-7-presentation-design-trends-dominating-2026-data-backed/); [Visible.vc: 11 trends 2026](https://visible.vc/blog/startup-presentation-design-trends/)
- Guides recommend diagrams over text, at most 6 bullets per slide, and one consistent color scheme. — [Reskilll](https://reskilll.com/blogs/sih-2026-ppt-template-exact-format-slides-evaluators-score/)

### Inferences (design recommendations; not sourced beyond the trends above)
- **Style options for SIH26170, ranked by fit:**
  1. **"Component datasheet" (most distinctive, on-theme).** Each slide looks like a space-grade part datasheet:
     - Part-number style header ("SIH26170 / REV A / SHEET 2 OF 6").
     - Thin-ruled tables and small-caps labels.
     - Parameter tables with Min/Typ/Max columns.
     - Characteristic curves (parameter vs. burn-in hours at 0/24/96/168 h).
     - A revision/approval block in the footer.

     This fits QA inspectors and ESS engineers, and nobody else will use it.
  2. **"Mission control / telemetry."** Dark navy/charcoal background with monospace readouts:
     - Status LEDs (GO / WATCH / NO-GO) instead of pass/fail.
     - Sparkline strips per part.
     - A "T+24h" style time axis.

     Use a single amber or cyan accent plus red for anomalies. Risk: dark PDFs can print or project badly. Keep contrast high and consider a light variant.
  3. **Blueprint / engineering drawing.** Grid background, thin line diagrams, dimension-arrow annotations, and a title block. Good for the Technical Approach architecture slide.
  4. **Bento grid.** Use this for the Impact and Feasibility slides: 4–6 tiles, one metric per tile.
- **Hybrid suggestion:** a light "datasheet" base with a dark "telemetry panel" as the hero graphic on slides 2–3. The official 6 headings are set as datasheet section labels.
- **Visualizing the ML for this problem:**
  - A lot-distribution strip or violin plot with the 50µA spec limit line and the outlier part highlighted. This makes "in spec but anomalous" visible at a glance.
  - A drift fan chart showing 0/24 h observed values, forecasts to 96/168 h with prediction intervals, and the threshold line crossed early.
  - A SHAP-style waterfall or a plain "reason card" ("Flagged because: ΔIleak slope 3.1× lot P95; z-score vs lot = 6.2").
  - A confusion-matrix or cost tile emphasizing zero or low false negatives.
  - A left-to-right pipeline of five boxes (synthetic generator → features → outlier detector + drift regressor → risk fusion → QA console) with sample data shapes annotated on the arrows.
- **Typography:**
  - One grotesk sans for headings (e.g. Inter, IBM Plex Sans, Space Grotesk).
  - One monospace for data and IDs (IBM Plex Mono, JetBrains Mono).
  - Body text at 14pt or larger.
  - Embed fonts on PDF export.
- **Color:**
  - Neutral base plus one accent.
  - Reserve red or amber strictly for "anomaly/at-risk" so color carries meaning.
  - Pair color with shape or labels so the deck stays readable for colorblind viewers.
  - Optionally nod to the SIH saffron/green palette only in the logo area.
- **Tools:**
  - Figma or PowerPoint for layout, since the template is supplied as PPT and must be exported to PDF.
  - Matplotlib/Plotly exported to SVG for the real charts from the synthetic data (real charts beat mockups).
  - draw.io, Excalidraw or Mermaid for architecture diagrams. Excalidraw's hand-drawn look clashes with the datasheet style, so prefer draw.io or Figma there.
  - Canva works, but its templates are widely reused by other teams.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve Duarte / slide:ology material or dedicated sources on "datasheet", "mission control" or "Swiss grid" deck styles because of the tool budget. The style recommendations above are my synthesis, not cited guidance.

## 5. Showing ISRO and space context without violating logo rules

### Takeaway
ISRO's copyright policy lets teams reproduce material from isro.gov.in free of charge, as long as it is reproduced accurately, not used in a derogatory or misleading context, and the source is acknowledged. It does not specifically cover the ISRO logo. The safest approach is to name "ISRO" as the problem-statement organization in text, and not use the ISRO logo in a way that implies endorsement.

### Cited Findings
- "Material featured on this site belongs to the DOS/ISRO and the same may be reproduced free of charge in any format or media without requiring specific permission," provided it is reproduced accurately, not used in a derogatory or misleading context, and the source is prominently acknowledged. The permission does not cover third-party copyrighted material. The policy does not specifically address logos or emblems. — [ISRO Copyright Policy](https://www.isro.gov.in/Copyright_Policy.html)
- No official ISRO logo-usage guideline was found. Logo files are hosted on third-party sites such as [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Indian_Space_Research_Organisation_Logo.svg), which does not imply permission for endorsement use.

### Inferences
- Use the SIH logo, which the official template itself carries. Write "Problem by: Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO)" as text on the title slide.
- For space context, use your own vector drawings of a thermal chamber, a PCB/DUT board, or a burn-in rack, or credited ISRO public mission images with "Image: ISRO" captions. Avoid placing the ISRO logo as a co-brand, which could read as "misleading context", and avoid the State Emblem entirely.
- Cite domain standards on the References slide (e.g., MIL-STD-883 burn-in, ESCC/ECSS screening, JEDEC) to show you understand space-grade screening. Other researchers should verify the exact standard numbers.

### Gaps
- No explicit ISRO logo policy was found. Restrictions on the Indian State Emblem (under the State Emblem of India (Prohibition of Improper Use) Act, 2005) are from background knowledge and were not verified in this session.
