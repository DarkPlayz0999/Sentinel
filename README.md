# SENTINEL

**AI-driven anomaly detection in component burn-in and screening.**

An explainable screening layer that catches *latent defects* in high-reliability
electronic components — parts that pass every datasheet limit but are quietly
degrading — before they are soldered into a payload that cannot be repaired.

> SENTINEL learns each production lot's own normal behaviour, detects components
> whose parametric drift is anomalous relative to their peers, and forecasts a
> part's 168-hour degradation from its first 24 hours of burn-in — so marginal
> parts are rejected early instead of failing in orbit.

Full technical spec: [`docs/SENTINEL_Burn-In_Anomaly_Detection_Blueprint.pdf`](docs/SENTINEL_Burn-In_Anomaly_Detection_Blueprint.pdf).
Read it before proposing architecture changes.

> **All data in this repository is simulated** and must be labelled as such in
> any submission. It is generated by `src/generate_burnin_dataset.py` with a
> fixed seed, which is what lets us prove recall against known ground truth —
> something real unlabelled burn-in data can never do.

---

## The problem

Components destined for space, defence, medical implants and automotive safety
go through burn-in: powered and operated at 125 °C, with electrical parameters
read at 0 h, 24 h, 96 h and 168 h. Screening today is judged on **static
datasheet limits**. A part is good if its standby current stays under, say,
50 µA.

The flaw is obvious once stated. If a lot's parts all sit around 10 µA and one
part climbs steadily from 12 µA to 32 µA over 168 hours, that part is screaming
that something is physically wrong inside it — a partially-open bond, a
resistive via, ionic contamination, gate-oxide damage — yet it ships, because
32 < 50.

In the shipped dataset, **100 % of latent defects pass every static datasheet
limit at 168 h**. Static screening catches none of them.

With an activation energy of 0.7 eV, 168 hours at 125 °C is roughly 18 years of
operation at 25 °C (Arrhenius acceleration factor ≈ 937). A drift visible in a
week of burn-in is a drift that will consume the part's entire mission life.

## The approach

| Module | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| **A — dynamic outlier detection** | Is this part behaving like its siblings? Robust statistics computed *per lot*, not against the datasheet. |
| **B — drift forecasting** | Where will this part be in a week? Predicts `Value_168h` from `Value_0h` and `Value_24h` only, then rejects early if the predicted slope exceeds a computed safety slope. |
| **Fusion + explainability** | Why? A 0–100 risk score that is a weighted sum of *named* sub-scores, plus numbered reason codes in inspector-facing English. |

Because Module B needs only the first 24 hours, a lot can be triaged at hour 24
— freeing **144 oven-hours per rejected part** on a scarce, power-hungry,
capacity-limiting resource.

## Generated data

`src/generate_burnin_dataset.py` produces 2,100 parts across 6 lots, 4
parameters, 4 read points, with the physics encoded as
`X(t) = X₀ · (1 + A · (t/168)ⁿ)`.

| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| `data/burnin_wide.csv` | one row per part, all read points, labels |
| `data/burnin_long.csv` | tidy long form for time-series work |
| `data/public_train.csv` | 60 % split, keeps labels and 168h values |
| `data/hidden_test.csv` | 40 % split, labels and 168h values stripped |
| `data/ground_truth.csv` | the stripped columns, for scoring |

Deliberate realism that makes the problem hard: lognormal currents and normal
timing; lot-to-lot offsets with one deliberately bad lot (**L04** — shifted
centre, 1.45× spread, 3× the defect rate); ~10 % of healthy parts naturally
"wide" so the tails genuinely overlap; each latent defect affecting only one or
two of the four parameters, so a detector watching only Iddq misses half the
population; 1.5 % measurement noise and a few dropped 96 h reads.

### Parameters

| Column | Distribution | Lot centre | USL | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Iddq_uA` | lognormal | 10.0 | 50.0 | quiescent supply current — the most sensitive CMOS defect indicator |
| `Ileak_nA` | lognormal | 20.0 | 200.0 | junction/insulator leakage — dielectric degradation, moisture ingress |
| `Tpd_ns` | normal | 3.20 | 4.60 | propagation delay — resistive interconnect, electromigration, NBTI |
| `Vol_mV` | normal | 210.0 | 400.0 | output low level — drive-transistor weakening, contact resistance |

Column convention: `{PARAM}_{HOURS}h`, e.g. `Iddq_uA_168h`. Labels are
`true_class` (`healthy` / `latent` / `gross`), `is_latent_defect` (the target),
and `static_fail_168h`. **Agree feature names before writing features** — half
of all hackathon merge conflicts are column names.

## Rules that are not negotiable

Summarised from `CLAUDE.md`, which is the authority. Read it in full.

**Domain**

1. Currents are **lognormal** — log-transform before any statistic. Timing and
   voltage are ~normal, use raw.
2. Never mean/std for limits or outlier scores. Use `median` and
   `1.4826 * MAD`. Mean-based limits get masked by the outliers they hunt.
3. All statistics are computed **per lot**. Lot-to-lot offsets are real and are
   the entire reason dynamic limits beat static ones.
4. Higher is worse for every parameter here. One-sided logic is fine — say so
   in the comment.

**ML**

5. **Never report plain accuracy.** ~8 % of parts are defective; predicting
   "all good" scores 92 %. Report recall, precision, F-β (β ≥ 2), PR-AUC, and
   recall at a stated overkill budget.
6. Validation splits are grouped by lot — `GroupKFold(groups=lot)`. A random
   part-level split leaks lot statistics and inflates every score.
7. Thresholds minimise `C_FN·FN + C_FP·FP`, with `C_FN/C_FP = 100` by default.
   The ratio is a parameter and a UI slider, never a magic number.
8. Feature logic lives **only** in `src/features.py`, imported by both training
   and inference.
9. Baseline before sophistication. A new model ships only if it beats the
   current baseline on the grouped holdout, and the baseline stays in the repo.
10. Deep learning is not the default. On ~2,000 tabular rows, robust statistics
    and gradient boosting win.

**Explainability** — a third of the marks

11. The top-level verdict is a **weighted sum of named sub-scores**, never a raw
    model output. ML improves the sub-scores; it does not make the decision.
12. Every flag emits a reason code (R-101 … R-601) in inspector-facing English
    containing the actual value *and* the lot reference value.

## Definition of done

- Runs end to end from a clean `data/` directory.
- Has a test if it touches `features.py` or `evaluate.py`.
- Reports its metric via `src/evaluate.py`, not an ad-hoc calculation.
- If it changes a number that appears in the slides, says so explicitly.

## Baseline to beat

Measured on the generated dataset (2,100 parts, 6 lots, 174 latent defects, 63
gross failures). **Reproduced in this repo** — `python src/baseline.py`
regenerates every figure below through `src/evaluate.py`.

| Method | Recall (latent) | Precision | F₂ | Parts flagged |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Static datasheet limits only | 0.000 | — | 0.000 | 55 (2.6 %) |
| Dynamic PAT, robust \|z\| ≥ 8 | 0.414 | 0.533 | 0.433 | 135 (6.4 %) |
| Dynamic PAT, robust \|z\| ≥ 6 | 0.523 | 0.555 | 0.529 | 164 (7.8 %) |
| Dynamic PAT, robust \|z\| ≥ 4.5 | 0.638 | 0.514 | 0.609 | 216 (10.3 %) |

Baseline PR-AUC: 0.3889. The four DPAT rows match blueprint §12 to three
decimal places, which is what validates `features.py` against the reference
implementation shipped with the spec.

> **One correction to §12, and it affects the slides.** The blueprint's table
> records static limits flagging "63 (gross only)". The real count is **55**:
> eight of the 63 gross parts do not breach any datasheet limit at 168 h — the
> worst of them reaches 96.9 % of its USL and still ships. Static screening is
> therefore slightly *worse* than the blueprint claims. The headline number is
> unaffected: 100 % of latent defects pass static limits, and static limits
> flag zero non-gross parts.

Recall against the over-rejection budget, measured on healthy parts only
(flagging a gross failure is correct behaviour, not overkill):

| Overkill budget | Recall |
|---|---|
| 1 % | 0.586 |
| 2 % | 0.638 |
| 5 % | 0.764 |
| 10 % | 0.839 |
| 20 % | 0.874 |

**Target: recall above 0.85 at an overkill rate under 10 %, with every flag
explained.** Univariate robust z plateaus around 0.84 — roughly a third of
latent defects are subtle in every single dimension and visible only as a
combination, so getting past the plateau needs the L3 multivariate layer.

The curvature view earns less than the blueprint predicts. It lifts recall at a
fixed threshold (0.414 → 0.448 at |z| ≥ 8) but leaves PR-AUC unchanged at
0.3889 to four decimal places, so it reorders nothing — it only shifts where a
given threshold lands. Treat it as a reason-code signal, not a ranking gain,
until L3 is in place.

## Build order

Deliberate: the scorer comes before the models, because you cannot compare
models you have no scorer for.

1. `evaluate.py` — recall, precision, F₂, PR-AUC, recall-vs-overkill curve,
   cost-minimising threshold, grouped by lot. **With tests.**
2. `features.py` — the four robust-z views per parameter, per lot. **With
   tests.**
3. Module A L1 + L2 — reproduce and then beat the baseline table above.
4. Module B — the power-law forecast, MAE per parameter, safety slope.
5. Module A L3 — robust Mahalanobis (MinCovDet) on the delta vector.
6. `fusion.py` — the weighted risk score and three-band verdict.
7. `explain.py` — reason codes, the drift-vs-lot-envelope plot, SHAP.
8. `app/dashboard.py` — lot table, part drill-down, cost-ratio slider.
9. `api.py`, screening report PDF, `run_demo.sh`.

## Vocabulary

- **Latent defect** — passes all limits, carries a growing physical flaw. The
  target class.
- **Escape** — a defective part that ships. False negative. Catastrophic.
- **Overkill** — a good part rejected. False positive. Expensive but survivable;
  this is the currency you trade to buy recall.
- **Robust sigma** — `1.4826 · MAD`, or `IQR / 1.35`. Equals σ for normal data.
- **DPAT** — Dynamic Part Average Testing. Limits = robust median ± 6 robust
  sigma, per lot. The industry method we are extending.
- **PDA** — Percent Defective Allowable. If more than ~5 % of a lot fails, the
  lot is rejected. This caps how aggressive we can be.
- **Safety slope** — max drift rate a part may show and still be trusted:
  `min(mission-based, population-based)`.
- **Masking / swamping** — the two failure modes of mean-based outlier
  detection: an outlier inflates σ and hides itself, or drags the mean so good
  parts look bad. Robust statistics exist to prevent both.
